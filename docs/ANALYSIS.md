# Sentinel Observer — an independent analysis of the app against the paper

*Analysis date: 2026-09-06 · Repository state: `main` @ `942f0f9` · Paper: `docs/observer-paper.md` (416 lines, read in full)*

This document audits the Sentinel Observer along four axes the author asked for: (1) does the code back the paper's claims, (2) engineering quality, (3) a scientific stress-test of the architecture's ideas, and (4) what actually happens when the suites and benches are run. It is written to be argued with. Every finding cites a file and line in the tree at `942f0f9`; where a finding comes from reading rather than running, it says so.

The short version: the engineering is far more careful than a ten-day, 148-commit project has any right to be — typecheck is clean, the honesty gates are real, malformed LLM output cannot crash anything, persistence round-trips. But the paper describes a system that is roughly one layer more alive than the one that ships. The oscillator field does not contribute retrievable content (the project's own `docs/SCALING.md` §17 and §19–20 already show this, and the paper does not carry that finding into its narrative); the "measured handover" is pinned near zero in production by a placeholder constant; the Boltzmann drive arbitration never routes anything; most §5.16 "landed" mechanisms are default-off or CLI-only; "acquires procedures" is one family (gcd) selected from ≤144 authored templates keyed on the drill's name; and several headline numbers rest on n ≤ 15, unseeded randomness, or a denominator that only counts distractors that answered. None of this makes the work uninteresting. It makes the paper, as written, overclaim relative to the code — and the fixes are mostly to the prose and the benches, not the architecture.

## 2. The substrate: what recall actually computes

This is the load-bearing section, because the paper's framing — "observation is entropy reduction", "thinking as coherence-making", "the observer's phase participates in its own measurement" — depends on the oscillator field contributing something to memory that a plain index would not.

**The recall score.** `CompactMemoryBank.scoreCandidates` (`packages/sentient-core/src/semantic/CompactMemoryBank.ts:678–835`) prefilters candidates by prime intersection (an inverted index, `candidatesFor`) and scores each as `0.5·smfGate·cos(SMF_cue, SMF_trace) + 0.5·overlap(primes)`. The phase term is `phaseTerm: 'off'` in production (`:381`) — dropped by the author's own §4.2 measurement (sibling AUC 0.495, chance). So retrieval is two terms.

**The overlap term** is a normalized amplitude overlap over the cue's prime set (`amplitudeOverlap`). Every vocabulary word has a deterministic, collision-salted 4-prime signature (`§3.1 Symbolic grounding`). For the recognition benchmark the cue *is the word itself* (`apps/web/src/teacher/deck.ts:38–40`, `agent/wordloop.ts:404–407`), so the overlap term is an exact-match hash lookup that cannot fail by construction.

**The SMF term** is the cosine between two 128-dim sketches. Each sketch is a seeded signed random projection of the vector `x_j = a_j·cos(φ_j)` over the 256 oscillators (`SedenionMemoryField.ts:463–509`), blended into the previous sketch by an EMA with `α = 0.2·(0.5+0.5·coherence)` (`:496–506`). Three facts about how it is fed:

1. `settleField()` resets the oscillators (all phases to a common start) but **not the sketch** (`SemanticObserver.ts:1451–1474`; `smfMomentImprint` defaults off). Every teach settles → excites → ticks 5 times (`agent/wordloop.ts:819–826`) → stores. At store, roughly `0.8^5 ≈ 33%` of the sketch is still the previous lessons' trajectory.
2. Because phases are reset to the same value before every lesson and then advance deterministically at prime-derived natural frequencies for the same number of ticks, the `cos(φ_j)` factors at store time are the **same fixed per-prime constants for every word**. The content part of the sketch is therefore a fixed linear map of the excited-prime amplitude vector — the same information the overlap term already scores, projected densely.
3. Quiz-time recall (`recallWithCue`, `wordloop.ts:419–428`) does *not* settle: it excites the cue on top of whatever the field and sketch already hold.

The project measured exactly this. `docs/SCALING.md` §19d: "Two traces taught BACK TO BACK are 0.919 similar regardless of content, against a 0.436 floor for unrelated traces … a trace's sketch is not its content: it is the observer's slowly-drifting trajectory at the moment it was taught." §20: replacing the EMA with a per-moment imprint "decollapses the sketch harder than anything measured so far … and yet destroys ranking (top-1 62.6%)" — i.e. **the recency trajectory is what retrieval ranks by**. §17e: at a settle depth where the field genuinely enters partial synchronization, "the UNCOUPLED field at the same depth raises [the margin] MORE … Coupling contributes nothing to the improvement." §17f: "**this field does not have a chimera regime to gate on.**"

Put together with §4.2 ("the moment carries no content beyond the excitation"), the project's own record says: the Kuramoto dynamics are causally inert for retrieval; the SMF term is a recency clock plus a dense re-projection of the prime bag; the phase term is off. What remains is an inverted-index nearest-neighbour search over hashed 4-prime signatures, with a recency prior. That is a perfectly respectable memory. It is not what §3.1 and §6 describe ("the field's agreement on the current system state", "perturbation and convergence … thinking as coherence-making … drives as resonance targets … The observer's 'thoughts' are its moments").

Two direct consequences for the headline numbers:

- **Recall 94.6% at 20k words is a self-retrieval rate under cross-talk from the recency term.** Pure prime overlap on collision-free signatures would score 100%; the 5.4% loss is plausibly *caused* by the SMF term, not mitigated by it. This is a cheap ablation (`smfWeight: 0` in `CompactMemoryBankOptions`) that the repository has never run at deck scale. It should be the first experiment after this document.
- **The §5.2 settle window (settle 0: 68% FP; settle 4: 0%; settle 6: exact recall collapses) is an artifact of two rate constants** — the sketch EMA (`α≈0.2`) and the amplitude decay — not of "convergence to agreement". At settle 0 the sketch is ~80% the previous utterance; at settle 6 the excitation has decayed below the index threshold. The paper presents this as "the convergence window is real and measurable" (§5.2). It is a measurable window, but it is a window in an EMA, and it would exist with the coupling constant set to zero.

To be clear about what this does *not* say: the entropy/coherence readings are real numbers computed from a real Kuramoto model (`PrimeOscillatorField.tick`, `tinyaleph`), and the author has been scrupulous about running the refuting experiments (§17, §19–20, §4.2, Hebbian WSD, field-level synthesis). The problem is that the paper's §3.1 and §6 prose has not been updated to what those experiments found.

## 4. How the headline numbers were produced

Every §5 number except the FSRS formula checks and the gated unit benches comes from `apps/web/src/cli/train.ts` flags or `scale-bench.ts`, is **printed, never asserted**, and has no log or manifest in the repository. The unit gates that do exist are deliberately loose (recall floors at 0.70 while the paper reports 94–99%; several benches are "verdict reported, not hard-gated" — `phaseFrameBenchmark.test.ts:37`, `shardRouteBenchmark.test.ts:70`; `cdeBenchmark` asserts only AUC ∈ [0,1]). No `.skip`/`.only` anywhere — good.

Sample sizes behind the headlines: honesty **44/44** = 4 chains × 3 targets × 3 predicates (36) + 3 unknown + 3 garbage + 2 shell probes (`train.ts:1313–1393`); chain **16/16** = 5 chains × 3 + 1; operators **6/6** and **2/2**; self-sufficiency **14/14** from a fixed 17–18-prompt mix truncated to 14 (`:984–1028` — the two adversarial prompts are positions 15–16); graph-silent **14/14**; fabrication **0/5**; settle-6 **0/12**; hybrid unknowns **60%** (n ≤ 10); definition quality **0.83** (n = 6); hypernym recovery **1.00** (n = 3 hand-authored hypernyms). Nothing here is wrong to report, but the abstract states them as if they were population rates.

Specific methodological problems, each verified in code:

**Fuzz 68% → 0% (§5.2, the abstract's lead number).** `train.ts:940–979`: fillers are drawn with unseeded `Math.random()` (not reproducible); the denominator `distractorCount` increments only when the distractor produced *any* response, so "0/15" is 0 of the 15 distractors that answered, out of 36+ asked, and the settle-0 and settle-4 arms have different denominators for that reason; n = 12 conversation pairs. Meanwhile `settleCriterion.test.ts:130–135` runs the same guard-free protocol on 120 pairs and gets **140 false positives at settle 4** — the paper reports 140→224 in §5.16 without reconciling it with §5.2's 0%. The reconciliation is that the chat identity gate, not settling, is what holds FP at 0.

**Self-referential benches.** The chain bench draws its probes from `teacher.relations()` and the operator answers by walking the same graph (`train.ts:1283`) — it checks operator/graph consistency, not truth. Adversarial negative targets are excluded if they fall inside the observer's *own* is-a closure (`adversarial.ts:40–`): a wrong edge removes the probe that would catch it, which is exactly how 109 confident cross-sense "Yes" answers stayed invisible to the 44/44 bench until the polysemy work found them (§5.16 §7). `p1-relations` derives its "derivable" set from the loose extractor and builds the hologram from the same extraction. `grounded-bench` grades fabrication with `criticize()`, the function that already gated the sentence. The calibration result 0.311 → 0.008 evaluates the isotonic fit **in-sample**. The retention simulation reviews *every* due word daily with the identity cue on a ~99%-recall recognizer, so 100/100 is close to tautological (and reported 0% until a snapshot bug was fixed — `TODO.md:214–220`).

**Recall is identity lookup.** The §5.1 table quizzes each word with itself as cue (§2 above). The genuinely hard faculty — recall from meaning — is the semantic-recall gate at 0.6 with a measured 85% paraphrase→word (`TODO.md:82`) and is absent from §5.

**Drift between the record and the paper.** `improvements-tasks.md:74` records the full-set H̃ AUC as 0.443; `cde.ts:36` and the paper say 0.510. Phase-frame AUC in `improvements-tasks.md:145–148` is 0.500/0.500 and 0.510/0.512; the paper's 0.495/0.497 appears only in comments. Constants count 105 in the tasks doc vs 124. None of these change conclusions; all of them mean the paper is being maintained by hand against a moving codebase.

## 5. Findings by subsystem

### 5.1 The conversational stack

`chatAnswer` is ~500 lines (`TeacherAgent.ts:575–1076`) and `applyOperator` ~735 (`operators.ts:426–1160`). The routing is real and mostly as described, with the qualifications in §3. Three things deserve attention beyond the table.

The ask layer can assert. `speakFromFrames` at `TeacherAgent.ts:1051–1057` speaks grounded frames about a read-about entity under `mode: 'ask'`, so a factual claim is counted as "abstained" by the deviation meter. Inherited has-part/capable-of/requires/has-property answers say "Yes —" unconditionally: `strength` is computed and then unused in the `via` branch (`operators.ts:579–587, 826–834`), so a weakened edge that should hedge does not when reached by inheritance. `COMPOSITION_RULES` includes `[has-part, capable-of] → capable-of` (`composition.ts:79`), which licenses "a house can open" from *house has-part door, door can open* — the table is labelled sound and is not.

English is handled by ~30 lead regexes (`operators.ts:91–148`). `LEAD_IS_NOT_A` (`:121`) matches "this is not a test", stores `(this, is-a, test)` in the confirmed-false store and *teaches* it as an exchange (`TeacherAgent.ts:728–742`). Pronoun resolution rewrites every pronoun globally (`context.ts:154`) before negation parsing, so "it is not a problem" after a turn about birds stores `(bird, is-a, problem)`. `evidenceInText` mines any non-question statement for corroboration (`corroboration.ts:173`). The evasion rule covers only the regex forms: "are robins birds" or "tell me, is a robin a bird" bypass it and compose.

Creative composition is smaller than the prose suggests. Grounded-first is 11 fixed hole-templates (`learnedFrames.ts:216–228`) filled from edge objects. The Markov fallback rebuilds bigram/trigram tables **per call** from ≤8 sentences (`agent/creative.ts:117–128`; `conversation.ts:465–578`) — a model of ~50–100 tokens — and "minimum-surprise search" is 24 random walks deduplicated and ranked (`conversation.ts:514–566`). That is sample-and-rank, and with ≤8 seeds it explains the 50% echo rate in §5.7 directly.

"Own-generation 100%" is relative to an LLM-seeded world: learned operators are induced only from LLM-graded ≥0.7 answers; chaperone edges are first-class "stored edges" for the critic; stored hybrid drafts become `kind: 'creative'` traces that compete in the memorized layer (`agent/conversation.ts:108–115`).

### 5.2 Relations, hologram, MDL

The typed graph, provenance, confirmed-false store and hypothesis tier are solid and well tested. The hologram is a correct FHRR implementation whose *input* is the loose extraction the hypothesis tier already holds — it adds crosstalk, not coverage, and its bench is a toy. The MDL learner is correctly implemented against a mis-specified cost model (slot counted as saved); the consequence is that it promotes fastest on the weakest evidence (unknown slot words), which is the opposite of the intent stated in §3.6.

### 5.3 The rewrite engine

The engine (`rules/engine.ts`) is the cleanest module in the repository: deterministic, genuinely call-by-value with a lazy conditional, fuel-bounded, with exhaustion mapped to ask. The authored decks total 62 rules (Peano 24, digits 14, int 9, logic 10, alg 5). The exactness guards ("7 / 2", "13 percent of 20", "3·x = 10", √50, ties — `reviewFixes.test.ts:28–40`) and take-away refusals (`stories.test.ts:145–170`) are real and tested.

The acquisition story is where the paper and code diverge most. The honest sentence is: *a bounded term rewriter over ~60 authored rules, with a template-matching acquisition path validated for one recursive function*. Specifically, "induction" is family-name → schema → first-consistent-of-≤144-templates; instruction parses one sentence shape for one family; the chaperone channel is unwired; positional arithmetic is unreachable; the logic deck's inference is done by the parser; MDL cannot fail; and the held-out validation for chaperone proposals uses a fixed seed (`0x7a07`, `chaperone.ts:213`) against the family's oracle — an oracle check, not a hold-out relative to anything the proposer saw. `rewriteInduction` defaults to `false` (`agent/base.ts:142`) and the server passes `true` with a stale comment claiming no drills run server-side (`ServerSession.ts:193–195`; they do, `autonomous.ts:166`).

The general story parser is on (`parse.ts:312`) and is a two-numbers-plus-keyword heuristic (`:270–304`). Plausible misreads: "Sam has 10 apples and gives 3 to Mia" → 13 (no "away"); "A box has 12 eggs and 3 are broken. How many good eggs?" → 15; "The zoo had 15 lions and 7 died. How many now?" → 22 ("now" is deliberately excluded from the residual net, `:293–294`). These are confident wrong answers of exactly the class §3.9 says the boundary is pinned against. Also `matchArgs` requires generator phrasing (`^what is (\d+) \+ (\d+)\??$`) — "What is 7 plus 5?" never reaches the engine.

### 5.4 Teacher loop, FSRS, trust

The FSRS curve is the FSRS-4.5 forgetting curve exactly (`retention.ts:31–35`); the scheduler around it is a hand-rolled homage (no S-dependence in growth, fixed ±0.1/+0.4 difficulty steps) — accurate to call it "FSRS-style", as the paper does. Wall-clock scheduling is real. The two defects are the never-called `applyRetention` in the server (strengths decay only on restart) and the restore-ordering bug that leaves n-gram and drive weights undecayed forever.

The trust kernel's formulas are exactly as written, which makes the `resonance = 0.5` placeholder more consequential, not less: the machinery is correct and is fed a judge that cannot agree. Separately, composite `novelty` (1 − token overlap with seeds) and the rule band's `grounding` (content-word overlap with seeds) are both lexical functions of the same (answer, seeds) pair — they are not independent judges even in principle, so "trust as measured agreement against ground truth" is measuring one overlap statistic against another.

### 5.5 Server and persistence

Default store JSON; SQLite optional (§3). The JSON store writes temp+rename (`FilePersistenceStore.ts:44–54`) without `fsync`, synchronously. With `persistEvery = 1` (`ServerSession.ts:186`) and a 4 s max delay (`support.ts:42`), the server re-serializes and sync-writes the entire ~20k-trace record every ≤4 s, plus a 30 s full snapshot in which `JSON.stringify(record)` is executed twice (`ServerSession.ts:536, 538`). Those are event-loop stalls that block every HTTP request. `saveNow` awaits `flush()` before `exportBootstrap` (`:532–533`), so `model.json` and the store can disagree.

The HTTP surface has no authentication and `access-control-allow-origin: *` (`http.ts:49, 60, 72, 125`). Any LAN client can `POST /api/teach` an arbitrary cue/response with no validation (`http.ts:231–243`; the LLM path is charset/length-checked at `chaperone.ts:126–142`, the human path is not), `/api/record/import` arbitrary traces (additive, no rollback on a mid-loop TypeError — `persistence.ts:756–768`), `/api/train` to stop training, or grow the unbounded, persisted `authoredAnswers` map via `/api/compose` (`agent/base.ts:462`). A 1 MB `/api/chat` body runs synchronous recall — trivial CPU denial of service. No crash path was found (handlers are try/caught). For a single-user local server this is acceptable; the paper's "deployment" language (§3.8) implies more.

Minor: the boot-time `TrainingLoop` omits `researchTopics` (`ServerSession.ts:234–240` vs `:266–273`), so `--research-topics` is inert until training is toggled through the API. The round-trip persistence test uses `MemoryPersistenceStore`, not the file or SQLite store (`persistenceRoundTrip.test.ts:32`).

## 7. Engineering review

**Shape.** ~60k lines of non-test TypeScript (excluding the 20k-line deck), 34k lines of tests in 157 files, 148 commits since 2026-08-28. `TeacherAgentCore` (`agent/base.ts`) carries 82 protected fields and is composed from 11 stacked mixins (`TeacherAgent.ts:198–210`) with `as unknown as TeacherAgent` casts; the constructor takes 16 positional parameters with trailing booleans (`:351–445`). `persistence.ts` is 1,198 lines of hand-written `typeof` validation that a schema library would replace. Two parallel executors (compiled DSL on JS numbers; rewrite engine on terms) share one regex grammar but the logic prompts have a second grammar (`logic.ts:159–222` vs `dsl.ts:399–418`) that disagrees on syllogism tails. Duplicated `freeVars` (`rules/types.ts:110–121` vs `terms.ts`), unused exports (`registerInducedRules`, `conversionFamilyOf`, `lookup`/`concat` DSL ops, `tSym` import in `engine.ts:23`), hot-path `isStopped → independentDenials` full scan per redex (`types.ts:179–180`).

**Tests.** Strong on unit coverage of the honesty gates; weak on the *claims*. The project distinguishes gates from benches sensibly (the 13 ignore patterns), but the benches that produce the paper's numbers are print-only CLI runs with no stored output. Eighteen tests sleep on real timers; one file uses fake timers. Timing assertions appear in unit suites. The suite is platform-locked by native binaries in a checked-in `node_modules` (see §1).

**Comments as documentation.** The code is exhaustively commented, and much of the paper's prose is recognizably the code comments. That has a cost: where a comment describes intent ("marks the sentence ungrounded", "no drills run server-side", "inherits corroboration") and the code does the opposite, the paper inherits the error. At least four of the defects in §6 are comment-says / code-does divergences.

**Housekeeping.** `apps/web/public/` holds 14 `bootstrap.json.bak-*` files (≈575 MB, gitignored — fine, but a rotation policy would help on a laptop); `apps/web/data/` is 215 MB of JSON re-written every few seconds by a running server. The `.kilo/worktrees/` copy of the tree (a stale branch) doubles every grep hit for anyone exploring the repo.

**Positives, stated plainly because they are real.** Typecheck clean under strict TS; error handling in the server and chaperone is defensive and correct; malformed LLM output cannot enter memory or crash the loop; persistence round-trips every learned store (beliefs, weights, kernel evidence) and the test asserts it; every experiment that failed was kept behind a default-off flag with its bench and its negative result written down. That last habit is rarer than it should be and is the reason this analysis could be written from the repository's own records.

## 8. Scientific stress-test

**What the architecture is.** Strip the vocabulary and the system is: a hashed sparse-signature nearest-neighbour memory with an EMA recency prior; a typed relation graph extracted by regex from WordNet glosses plus LLM-supplied edges, walked with bounded transitivity/inheritance; a small set of English-regex operators; a template learner with an MDL-shaped gate; a term rewriter over ~60 authored rules with a template-selection acquisition path; an FSRS-4.5 scheduler; and an LLM that writes curriculum and grades. Each piece is competent. The paper's contribution claims live in the connective tissue — entropy reduction, moments, resonance, drives, trust, self-modeling — and that is where the code is thinnest.

**Where the field earns nothing yet.** §2 above. The honest position the project's own data supports is: the oscillator field is currently a *randomized feature encoder* (a fixed cos-weighted projection) plus a clock. Kuramoto coupling is inert for retrieval (§17e), has no chimera regime at this sparsity (§17f), and Hebbian coupling refutes as WSD at this scale (§5.16). The question the paper needs to face directly is whether the field is a hypothesis about memory or a generator of pleasant metrics. The decisive experiments are cheap: (a) `smfWeight: 0` ablation at 5k/20k — if recall rises, the SMF term is noise; (b) `coupling: 0` at production settle depth on every heavy gate — if nothing moves, coupling is decorative; (c) a matched-cost baseline (an inverted index over 4-prime signatures plus a 128-dim random projection of the prime bag, no dynamics) reporting the same tables. If the observer beats (c), the paper has a result; if not, the "field" sections should be rewritten as an encoding, and the honest contribution becomes the graded symbolic stack.

**Where the learning actually happens.** Facts: the relation graph, supplied by extraction and by the LLM. Behaviours: template shells, promoted by (mis-specified) MDL from LLM-graded exchanges. Procedures: one recursive function selected from authored templates. Weights: n-gram overlays that never decay in production; drive weights that are never consulted by any arbitration. Trust: pinned. So "the observer owns its memory; the LLM is a gated faculty" is accurate, and "the observer contributes competence, not just memory" (§6.1) is defensible only for the chain walk and the rewrite engine — both of which are hand-authored algorithms over stored data, not acquired competence.

**The mind section (§6.1).** Defining a mind by three architectural capacities and then locating the system "on the substrate" is a rhetorical move the code does not support: belief traces exist, but the evaluative gradient (drive weights) is never read by a chooser, goals have no production loop, and the "reflexive self-response" (answers written to working memory) is a ring buffer of eight turns feeding pronoun resolution and Markov seeds. A skeptical reviewer will read §6.1 as the paper's weakest section by a wide margin, and it currently occupies more space than the results it depends on. It would be stronger as one paragraph that names the three capacities and states flatly that none is operational.

**The council (§5.12, A.5–A.6).** "Integration by resonance" is: ask three observers; if one answers grounded, take it; else re-excite each with the others' texts and check token/edge overlap. Entropy of the response distribution over three agents is a small number that falls when they agree by construction. The niche result (a diagonal contributor matrix from three domain-partitioned decks) is what a router over disjoint vocabularies must produce. This is fine as a first plumbing test and should be described as one.

**What the paper undersells.** The exactness guards and take-away refusals are exactly the kind of boundary discipline that most symbolic-QA demos skip. The negative results (§4.2, §17, §19–20, shard routing, field-level synthesis, Hebbian WSD, candidate-entropy routing) are a genuine contribution — most projects bury them. The persistence design and the "everything is a trace under one law" *intent* is coherent even where the implementation has not caught up. The rewrite engine is small and right. The sense-split work found a real fabrication class the honesty bench could not see, which is the strongest argument in the paper for building adversarial benches that do not consult the model's own graph.

## 9. Recommendations, in order

1. **Run the three ablations in §8** before any further mechanism work: `smfWeight: 0`, `coupling: 0`, and a no-dynamics baseline, on the existing heavy gates. Report whatever they say in §3.1/§5.1 and rewrite §6's substrate paragraphs to match.
2. **Fix defects 1–5** (resonance placeholder, two retention-decay bugs, critic clause drop, MDL slot accounting). Each changes a claim; together they are a day's work. Then re-measure λ, fabrication rate, and the operator audit.
3. **Make the benches reproducible and honest about n.** Seed the fuzz bench, count all distractors, store every bench's output as a committed JSON artifact with the commit hash, and have the paper's tables generated from those artifacts. State n next to every rate. Move at least one honesty bench off the model's own closure (hand-authored negatives, or WordNet hypernym distance).
4. **Separate "landed" from "on".** Add a one-page table to the paper (or `docs/FLAGS.md`) listing every mechanism, its flag, its production default, and its bench. Right now a reader cannot tell that elaboration, concept synthesis, calibrated gates, sense split and path hedging are off.
5. **Rewrite §3.9 to its true scope** — one inducible family from ≤144 templates, one instruction shape, chaperone path unwired — or wire the missing pieces and make induction a real body search (the design in `RULE_LEARNING.md:331–343` is already written). Fix the logic parser or remove the MT/syllogism families from the "derives with zero fabrication" claim.
6. **Close the honesty holes** in §6 rows 6–13, then add adversarial tests for idiom negations, pronoun-rewritten negations, non-generator arithmetic phrasing, and the story-parser misreads listed in §5.3.
7. **Engineering:** generate test counts from a run; remove timing assertions from unit suites (or gate them under an env var); add a `constants.test.ts` check that each registry `file:line` points at its symbol; unbuild the mixin tower into a few explicit collaborators (`Memory`, `Relations`, `Rules`, `Curriculum`, `Grading`) with an interface each; add `fsync` and a single serialization per snapshot; put a minimal bearer token on the HTTP surface; commit a lockfile-driven `npm ci` path instead of `node_modules`.
8. **Paper hygiene:** the abstract is one 600-word paragraph carrying every number in the paper; split it and drop the numbers whose n < 30 or whose bench is self-referential until they are re-run. Bring A.3 to the shipped record (1,650 B/trace, 55 MB). Reconcile §5.2's 0% with §5.16's 140.
